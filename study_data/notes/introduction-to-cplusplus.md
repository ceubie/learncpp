C (1972, Dennis Ritchie at Bell Labs) was a systems language: small, efficient, portable, lots of control over memory. Unix was rewritten in C, which helped both spread. Informal spec: K&R (1978). Formal standards: ANSI C / C89, then C90, later C99.

C++ (Bjarne Stroustrup, starting 1979) began as an extension of C. It is often treated as a superset of C, though that is not strictly true. The standout addition over C is support for object-oriented programming.

C++ was standardized by ISO in 1998. Informal names use the publication year: C++11, C++14, C++17, C++20, C++23. C++11 is widely treated as the modern baseline. New standards arrive about every three years.

Philosophy: “trust the programmer.” C++ gives you a lot of freedom and will not always stop you from doing something that does not make sense. Knowing what not to do matters as much as knowing what to do.

C++ is for high performance and precise control of memory/resources: games, real-time systems, embedded, audio/video, simulations, etc. You do not need to know C first.
